Why AI

Where this actually started.

The starting point is a Telugu film called Maharshi (2019), which I watched not long after finishing 11th grade. Its lead character, a graduate student, pitches an idea for an AI operating system to a company called Origin — it's accepted, and he ends up running the company. Years later, at the height of that success, he walks away from it to go help the farmers in his village instead. The film won a National Film Award, and what actually stuck with me wasn't the technology itself — it was AI showing up, for the first time, as something worth caring about.

That idea became concrete a little later, in a conversation with my brother, Rajesh. He was the one who pointed at a Google Assistant response and said, "that's AI" — and that's when what the movie had planted actually turned into something I could see working in front of me. I still remember it.

Between those two moments — the movie that made AI worth caring about, and the conversation that made it real — is roughly the shape of why I'm still here. It's why I ended up in research labs working on retinal imaging and organoid microscopy instead of just shipping features, and it's the same instinct behind the rest of this site.
